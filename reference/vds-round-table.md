## Description

For decades, efforts have been focused on deploying more and more sensors, platforms, and command centers. Today, recent conflicts have shown us that the real challenge lies in maintaining their capabilities when the adversary attempts to degrade their communications, manipulate information, or directly attack them, as they have become a primary target.

C4ISR systems can no longer be understood solely as command and control platforms; they are distributed digital systems and, like any digital system, they are a priority target for the adversary, who will not hesitate to use electronic warfare (EW) systems, cyberattacks, spoofing, jamming, and data manipulation to degrade their capabilities.

Superiority no longer depends solely on having more sensors or better communications, but on ensuring that information remains reliable even when everything is under attack.

## Questions

1. From Connectivity to Survival

   "**Víctor**, for years the challenge was connecting systems; today it’s keeping them connected under jamming and physical attack. How does this shift fundamentally change how BeeChat designs resilient tactical communication networks, and how do you ensure an operator can maintain operational tempo when data feed accuracy is compromised?"

2. Artificial Intelligence in C4ISR: Decision Superiority or Information Saturation?

   "**Gustavo**, we are integrating AI when the battlefield generates more raw data than ever. At XRF, how do you prevent cognitive overload? Specifically, what information should the system actively hide or suppress under fire, and where must the AI's recommendation end and human command begin when seconds matter?"

3. Technological Sovereignty & Regional Industrial Capabilities

   "**Felipe**, achieving European technological sovereignty requires a solid industrial foundation. From the Generalitat Valenciana's perspective, how can public policy actively support dual-use tech ecosystems so local innovators like BeeChat and XRF can scale, while ensuring regional suppliers build the cyber resilience needed"

4. Open Question in 1 minute

   "Before our final closing thoughts, I have an open question for all three of you.

   There is no technological sovereignty without innovation. In your experience, what role does innovation play in building true sovereign capabilities? And how is your organization investing in technology that is not just competitive, but strategically vital for Europe?"
